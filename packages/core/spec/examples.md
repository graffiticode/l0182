# L0182 RAG Training Examples

51 example prompts for training a RAG model on L0182, the collective-intelligence survey
language — covering idea sampling, bounded selection, ranking, contributing ideas to a shared
pool, showing the group's live results, mixed human and agent participation, and activity flow.

Each numbered line is a prompt in the author's own voice. Prompts describe WHAT to build,
never how to write it: an author asks for a survey, not for an `items [...]` program. When
L0182 grows further item kinds, this file grows categories with them.

## Category 1: Basic Survey Activities (1–10)

1. A survey asking people what our team should focus on next quarter. Show each person 10 ideas and let them pick up to 5.
2. Set up a collective-intelligence session for gathering product feedback from customers.
3. I want to run a brainstorm with my company about how to reduce meeting overload.
4. Create an idea-ranking activity for our neighbourhood association about local priorities.
5. A quick two-minute survey where people choose the issues that matter most to them.
6. Build an activity that shows people a sample of suggestions and asks them to pick their favourites.
7. Run a group ideation session on what to name our new product.
8. A survey for students about what would most improve the campus.
9. Gather ideas from the community about how to spend the parks budget.
10. An activity asking employees which workplace policies they would change.

## Category 2: Sampling and Selection Limits (11–18)

11. Show each participant 12 ideas and let them select exactly 3.
12. A survey where people see 10 suggestions and must pick at least 1 but no more than 5.
13. Draw a sample of 8 ideas and let people choose as many as they like.
14. Show 15 ideas but limit each person to their top 4.
15. Let people select between 2 and 6 ideas from a sample of 20.
16. A survey with a small sample — 5 ideas, pick 1.
17. Show 10 ideas and allow no selection at all if none appeal, up to a maximum of 5.
18. Sample 25 ideas per participant, with a cap of 10 selections.

## Category 3: Ranking (19–23)

19. After people pick their favourite ideas, have them drag those into order of importance.
20. A survey that asks people to select then rank their choices.
21. Let participants order their selected ideas from most to least important.
22. Add a ranking step with the instruction 'Put these in the order you care about them.'
23. People choose 5 issues and then rank those 5.

## Category 4: Contributing Ideas (24–29)

24. Let people add one idea of their own after they have voted.
25. A survey where contributing an idea is optional and can be skipped.
26. Ask participants to submit a single suggestion, one at a time.
27. Require every participant to contribute an idea before finishing.
28. Add an idea box with the note 'Please enter only one idea at a time.'
29. Collect one new suggestion per person to grow the pool.

## Category 5: Showing Results (30–35)

30. Show people the group's current ranking at the end.
31. Display the top 10 ideas with their scores.
32. Show the leading ideas and how many people have taken part.
33. A results screen showing only the top 5, without scores.
34. Let participants see how the group ranked everything after they finish.
35. Show the ranking but hide the participant count.

## Category 6: Humans and Agents (36–41)

36. A survey that both people and AI agents can take.
37. Run the activity with human participants only.
38. Let agents take the survey but show humans only the human ranking.
39. Open the session to agents as well as people, and show everyone the combined ranking.
40. A survey where the results screen shows the agent ranking separately.
41. Restrict participation to agents for a dry run before opening it to people.

## Category 7: Activity Settings and Flow (42–51)

42. A survey people cannot go back through once they have answered.
43. Let participants move freely between the items.
44. Add a welcome screen before the first question.
45. Finish with a thank-you message inviting people to come back.
46. A complete flow: welcome, choose, rank, contribute, results, thanks.
47. Submit each answer as it is given so people can resume where they left off.
48. Hold all the answers until the participant finishes the whole activity.
49. Title the activity 'You Can Choose' and point it at session abc123.
50. A minimal survey — just the selection step, nothing else.
51. An activity with a start screen and a results screen but no ranking.
